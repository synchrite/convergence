Convergence Bench

You are behind a colour television with its back off. The picture is in the mirror on the left, reversed. On the right is what your hands can reach: the ring magnets on the neck of the tube, the yoke, and the glass around it. Turn the tabs and watch the coloured lines slide over each other, as a technician does.

Hands. Drag a tab round the neck to move it. Drag the coloured band between a pair of tabs to turn both together. Turning a pair changes the direction it pushes the beams; spreading its two tabs apart changes how hard; tabs together cancel. Drag the dot in the YOKE pad to tilt the yoke, and the slider to move it along the neck. Keyboard: Tab chooses a ring, Left and Right turn the pair, Up and Down spread it.

Fitting a strip. There are two views of the same assembly; V changes between them. A convergence strip is a small flat magnet that goes between the front edge of the yoke and the funnel glass. 1. Rear view: click the glass at the place round the tube nearest the bad corner, and drag the strip round if it needs moving. 2. Side view: drag it left to push it further in under the yoke, watching the corner in the mirror; further in is stronger. 3. If the fringe gets worse, turn the strip over (F). 4. When the corner is white, tape it down (T). A loose strip moves, and the set comes back. X peels a strip off. There are four on the sheet.

The rings. Purity (2-pole) moves all three beams together: judge it on the red field. 4-pole moves red and blue in opposite directions: use it to lay red on blue (with green off they make magenta). 6-pole moves red and blue the same way: use it to lay that magenta on green. Green never moves. The rings act on the whole picture equally, so they are set on the centre. What is left at the edges is for the yoke, and what is left in a corner after that is for a strip.

Inspired by Charge! by C. Anthony Ray (BYTE, December 1982). See also inside the tube, the beam-path game this grew from, and the design specification. The model is a teaching model; a real chassis differs in detail. Never open a real set unless you are trained: it holds lethal voltages.
