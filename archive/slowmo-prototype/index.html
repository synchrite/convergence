Slow Motion CRT

One trip through a colour picture tube, from the hot cathode to the light leaving the screen, slowed until it can be watched. The whole trip takes about 4 billionths of a second. Five views, each closer than the last. The clock in the corner says how far time is slowed in the view you are looking at.

What is calculated and what is drawn

ViewCalculatedDrawn to help, not calculated
1 GunEnergy and speed of each electron from the voltage it has fallen through, with relativity. Transit time.The shape of the beam between the electrodes. The voltage along the axis is a smooth curve between typical electrode voltages, not a field solution.
2 FlightEach electron's path through a uniform magnetic field: a circle of radius momentum / (charge x field). Where each beam lands.The tube outline. A real yoke's field is shaped, not uniform, and a real screen is curved. Electrons stopped by the mask are not removed in this view.
3 MaskStraight-line paths from three guns through the slots to the stripes. Which electrons the mask stops, which stripe the rest reach, and the heating.Depth is compressed about 3 times against width so the angles can be seen. Slots are shown in one direction only.
4 PhosphorEach electron's path in the solid, by the single-scattering Monte Carlo method used in electron microscopy (screened Rutherford scattering, Bethe energy loss as modified by Joy and Luo). Energy left in each layer. Number of photons from published efficiencies.The grain outlines. The solid is treated as continuous. Light is drawn as straight rays; in a real screen it scatters between grains.
5 AtomsNothing: this view is a diagram.All of it. It shows the accepted sequence of events at the right sizes, with the real durations written on it.

The numbers are typical textbook values for a 1980s colour television tube (25 kV, in-line guns, slot mask, P22 phosphors), not measurements of one tube. Decay times and efficiencies vary between makers and are given to one significant figure. A phosphor is a crystal, so the smallest view shows atoms in a lattice, not molecules.

Never open a real set unless you are trained: it holds lethal voltages.
