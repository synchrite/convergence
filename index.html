Charge! Convergence

Three electron beams, red, green and blue, leave the gun and have to meet at one point on the screen. Stray charges bend each beam by a different amount. You are a technician standing behind a television with its back off. You cannot see the screen from here, so a mirror stands in front of the set, on the right. The picture in it has come apart into red, green and blue ghosts. Stick magnetic strips onto the tube until every character in the mirror snaps back into one sharp white picture. The first levels have one dot and one charge; later levels add dots, charges and a purity target.

Mouse: click inside the tube to place a strip, drag a strip to move it, click a strip to remove it, right click or R to rotate. Click a test dot to see its three beams. Keys: R rotate, P polarity, T dipole or quadrupole, 1 2 3 strength (these change the strip under the cursor, or the next strip), Tab next dot, arrows and Space place by keyboard, U undo, C clear, A auto-place, N next level, F filter.

What you are looking at. The big view is the set from behind and to one side: the electron gun and neck on the left with its coloured ring magnets, the copper deflection yoke, the glass funnel widening to the faceplate on the right, and the chassis below. The + and - marks are stray magnetic fields in the cabinet that bend the beams. On the far right is the mirror: it shows the front of the screen, reversed left to right, which is how convergence is really done, because the adjustments are at the back and the picture is at the front. Each character is drawn three times, once by each beam; when the beams land apart you see three coloured ghosts, and when they land together one white character that starts to dance. Only the three beams for the marked character are drawn: click a character to see its beams. CONV is the spread between the beams in pixels; PUR, on later levels, is how far the three together miss the white mark.

Descended from Charge! by C. Anthony Ray (BYTE, December 1982): the charges and their placement follow his listing. Other versions: Charge! Phosphor, the physics lab, the 1982 Applesoft listing.
