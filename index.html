Charge! Convergence

Three electron beams, red, green and blue, leave the gun and have to meet at one point on the screen. Stray charges bend each beam by a different amount. You are a technician standing behind a television with its back off. You cannot see the screen from here, so a mirror stands in front of the set, on the right. The picture in it has come apart into red, green and blue ghosts. Stick magnetic strips onto the tube until every character in the mirror snaps back into one sharp white picture. A short tutorial opens the first time you play, and the Tutorial button brings it back. There are three levels of ten stages: level 1 gives you one strip, level 2 two, level 3 three, and the neck rings arrive along the way. Three modes: in Easy a strip always stands upright and no stage needs it turned; in Medium it can be given a quarter turn; in Hard it turns through four angles.

Playing. Move the pointer over the beams: the strip under it already bends them, so you can see what it will do before you click. Click to stick it down, drag to slide it, click it again to take it off. Coloured arrows on a strip show which way it pushes each beam. The yellow line at the bottom says what is wrong with the marked picture. The APART bar must shrink inside the green mark. When a spot will fix the set, the strip turns green and the line says so. Rings (from stage 1-6): click < and > in the RINGS box, or Z and X, V for the next ring. Other keys: P flip, T squeezer or mover, R rotate, 1 2 3 strength (or the mouse wheel), Tab next picture, U undo, C clear, A auto-place, N next stage, F filter.

What you are looking at. The big view is the set from behind and to one side: the electron gun and neck on the left with its coloured ring magnets, the copper deflection yoke, the glass funnel widening to the faceplate on the right, and the chassis below. On the far right is the mirror: it shows the front of the screen, reversed, which is how convergence is really done, because the adjustments are at the back and the picture is at the front. Each character is drawn three times, once by each beam; apart, you see three coloured ghosts, together one white character that dances.

Why the picture is wrong. The small parts marked + and - on the glass are the real causes of misconvergence: an uneven winding in the yoke, a magnetised bracket or shield, a loudspeaker magnet. + pulls the beams toward it and - pushes them away (the marks are a nod to the charges of the 1982 game). They bend the near beam more than the far one, so the three land apart. You cannot remove them; you cancel them.

The rules a technician uses, and you can too. 1. A ring on the neck changes every picture by the same amount: set the rings first, on the middle picture. Squeeze moves red and blue opposite ways, shift moves them together, purity moves all three. 2. A strip only changes the beams that pass through it: use strips last, near the screen, for a picture the rings could not fix. 3. The further back a strip is, the more the beam has moved by the time it reaches the screen. 4. Green never moves: it is the reference. There are two kinds of strip: a squeezer (a convergence magnet) and a mover (a purity magnet).

How this relates to a real tube. A real tube is three-dimensional; this view is the flat slice through the three guns, which sit side by side in a row. On a real set the same rules apply a second time for up and down: each ring is turned to choose the direction and its two tabs are spread to choose the strength. See docs/REAL-CRT.md.
